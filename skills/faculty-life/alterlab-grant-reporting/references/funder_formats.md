# Funder Report Formats

Per-funder structure for post-award technical/progress reports. Use the funder's
own section letters and titles verbatim — funders reject reports that merge,
rename, or reorder sections. Every fact below is sourced inline.

---

## NIH — Research Performance Progress Report (RPPR)

Submitted in **eRA Commons**. One section skeleton (A–I) serves three report
types. Section titles are from the NIH RPPR Instruction Guide (eRA, January 2026).

| § | Section title | What it covers |
|---|---------------|----------------|
| A | Cover Page | PD/PI, organization, project title, reporting period |
| B | Accomplishments | B.1 major goals (goals = specific aims); B.2 what was accomplished (≤ 2 pages for most awards; include approaches that ensure robust, unbiased results); B.3 revisions/supplements; B.4 training and professional development (incl. use of IDPs for grad students/postdocs); B.5 dissemination; B.6 plans for the next period (not on Interim/Final) |
| C | Products | C.1 publications (incl. preprints) directly arising from the award — each must comply with the NIH Public Access Policy; C.2 websites; C.3 technologies/techniques; C.4 inventions/patents; C.5 other products, resource sharing, and **C.5.c Data Management and Sharing** progress per data type (generated? shared? repository, identifier) for awards under the 2023 DMS Policy |
| D | Participants | D.1 individuals who worked ≥ 1 person-month, plus any **Biographical Sketch / Current and Pending (Other) Support Common Forms** for new senior/key personnel or those whose other support changed (their ORCID must match the one linked to their eRA Commons ID) |
| E | Impact | Impact on the principal discipline, on other disciplines, on technology transfer, and on society |
| F | Changes | F.2 actual or anticipated challenges or delays and plans to resolve them; F.3 significant changes to human subjects, vertebrate animals, biohazards, select agents (F.1 applies only to some award types). **Annual RPPR only** |
| G | Special Reporting Requirements | G.1 Notice-of-Award/NOFO terms — for RPPRs submitted on or after 25 Jan 2026 this includes each senior/key person's annual certification of non-participation in a Malign Foreign Talent Recruitment Program (flattened PDF `MFTRPcert_[Name].pdf`); G.4 human subjects/inclusion; G.9 foreign component; **G.10 estimated unobligated balance** (explain it if > 25% of the current year's total approved budget, and how carryover would be spent); G.11 program income; G.12 F&A changes (SNAP) |
| H | Budget | Budget for the next period — **Annual non-SNAP only** |
| I | Outcomes | Concise, public-facing summary of project outcomes — **Interim/Final only** |

### Section applicability by report type

- **Annual RPPR** — describes scientific progress, significant changes, personnel,
  and plans for the next budget period/year. Section H (Budget) appears on
  **non-SNAP** annual reports. **MYF** awards (fully funded at the outset; NIH
  expanded this "forward funding" from FY2025, and the FY2026 appropriations act
  capped it at the FY2025 level) file an annual RPPR covering the year before each
  award anniversary; none is normally due during a no-cost extension unless the IC
  asks.
- **Interim RPPR** — filed when a Type 2 (competing) renewal is submitted. If the
  renewal is funded, it serves as the annual report for the final year of the
  prior competitive segment; if not funded, it becomes the Final report.
- **Final RPPR** — part of closeout. Interim and Final RPPRs share one format:
  **only D.1** is required in Section D, **Sections F and H are not part of
  them**, and **Section I (Outcomes)** is required. Report only the people who
  worked during the last budget period (excluding any approved no-cost extension).

What changed recently (RPPR Instruction Guide revision history):

- **Oct 2024** — C.5.c Data Management and Sharing progress reporting for RPPRs
  submitted on or after 1 Oct 2024 (NOT-OD-24-175).
- **May 2025** — B.3 instructions revised; the D.1 "Supplement Support" field is
  no longer required.
- **Jan 2026** — Section D adopts the Common Form Biosketch and Current and Pending
  (Other) Support; G.1 adds the annual MFTRP certification (NOT-OD-26-018). After a
  leniency period (NOT-OD-26-033), eRA has rejected RPPRs without compliant Common
  Forms since **8 May 2026** (NOT-OD-26-079); the forms are generated in SciENcv.
- **Public access** — the 2024 NIH Public Access Policy (NOT-OD-25-047) took
  effect 1 July 2025 (advanced by NOT-OD-25-101) for manuscripts accepted on or
  after that date: the author accepted manuscript goes to PubMed Central on
  acceptance and is public on the publication date, with no embargo.
  Non-compliant C.1 publications must still be listed, and fixing them generally
  takes weeks, which can delay the next award.

Sources:
- NIH RPPR Instruction Guide (eRA, January 2026) — sections A–I, Interim/Final
  differences, due dates (§3.1), revision history.
  https://grants.nih.gov/grants/rppr/rppr_instruction_guide.pdf
- eRA, "Research Performance Progress Reports (RPPR)",
  https://www.era.nih.gov/recipients/submit-reports/rppr.htm — the three report
  types and their purpose.
- NIH, "Instructions for Progress Reports for Multi-year Funded (MYF) Awards",
  https://grants.nih.gov/grants-process/post-award-monitoring-and-reporting/reporting-requirements/research-performance-progress-report-rppr/multi-year-funded-progress-report

### NIH RPPR deadlines (RPPR Instruction Guide §3.1)

- **SNAP** (Streamlined Non-competing Award Process) annual RPPR — due the **15th
  of the month preceding the month in which the budget period ends** (budget
  period ends 11/30 → due 10/15).
- **Non-SNAP** annual RPPR — due the **1st of the month preceding the month in
  which the budget period ends** (ends 11/30 → due 10/1).
- A due date on a weekend or federal holiday is automatically extended to the next
  business day.
- **Fellowships** — two months before the start of the next budget period.
- **Multi-Year Funded (MYF)** RPPR — due annually on or before the anniversary of
  the budget/project period start date; it covers the year preceding the
  anniversary.
- **Final RPPR** and **Interim RPPR** — due **within 120 days** of the end of the
  period of performance.
- A due date printed on the Notice of Award overrides all of the above.

## NSF — Project Reports

Submitted in **Research.gov**. Three report products:

- **Annual project report** — covers that year's activities related to the funded
  project plus broader-impacts work. Due **no later than 90 days before** the end
  of the current reporting budget period.
- **Final project report** — refers **only to the final funded year** of the
  project; it is **not** a cumulative report. Due **no later than 120 days
  following** the award end date.
- **Project Outcomes Report (POR)** — a summary of **no more than 800 words** on
  the nature and outcomes of the project, written for a **public audience**. Due
  **no later than 120 days following** the award end date. Separate from the final
  project report.

Current policy base (checked 2026-09-23): the PAPPG is still **NSF 24-1**; NSF
deferred a 26-1 edition and issues changes as supplemental policy notices —
**Supplement 1 (NSF 26-200)** and **Supplement 2 (NSF 26-202, awards issued on or
after 22 Jan 2026)**. Supplement 2 matters for reports:

- Journal articles (author accepted manuscript or version of record) go to the
  **NSF Public Access Repository (NSF-PAR)** on or before the official
  publication date — the 12-month embargo is gone.
- PAR identifiers and article/conference-paper metadata **auto-populate** the
  products section of Research.gov project reports.
- **Datasets can no longer be entered directly** into a project report; deposit
  them in a repository and report them from there.

Sources:
- NSF, "Report Your Progress and Outcomes",
  https://www.nsf.gov/awards/report-your-outcomes (PAPPG VII.D.1.a–b).
- Institutional sponsored-programs guidance (e.g. MIT RAS, WUSTL Financial
  Services) restating the 90-day / 120-day / 800-word rules.
- MIT RAS, "NSF PAPPG 24-1 Supplement 2 — Effective January 22, 2026", and
  Cornell Research Services, "NSF Public Access Policy" — NSF-PAR and project
  report changes.

## Horizon Europe / ERC — Periodic & Final Reports

Submitted on the **EU Funding & Tenders Portal** (Continuous & Periodic Reporting
modules). The report has two parts:

### Technical Report

- **Part A** — structured tables with project information; **generated by the IT
  system** from what the beneficiary enters into the Portal's Continuous &
  Periodic Reporting modules.
- **Part B** — the **narrative**; it mirrors the application form and requires the
  participants to **report on differences / deviations** from the planned work.

### Financial Report

- **Individual and consolidated Financial Statements** for each beneficiary.
- A **Certificate on the Financial Statements (CFS)** where the threshold applies.
- Report on the use of resources.

### Continuous Reporting module

Open throughout the project; information entered there transfers automatically to
the Periodic Reporting module (and feeds Part A).

### Lump-sum grants

Where the Grant Agreement uses a **lump sum**, the financial statement is generated
from the work packages declared complete (payment follows completed work
packages; partially completed ones can be declared pro rata at the final
period). There are no actual costs to report, and the use-of-resources
explanation and the CFS do not apply — the evidence burden moves to the technical
report showing each declared work package was genuinely completed.

### ERC specifics

The ERC runs **distinct scientific and periodic (financial) reports on different
interim periods**: in a typical 5-year Horizon Europe ERC grant the interim
(mid-term) scientific report is due after month 24 and the financial report after
month 30; the exact periods are in the Grant Agreement. For the **final**
period both reports are submitted together. Templates (Scientific Progress
Report; Technical Report Part B for the periodic report) are downloaded from the
project's Grant Management section; the periodic (financial) report does not
apply to lump-sum grants.

### Deadlines

- **Periodic report** (and the **final** periodic report) — due **within 60 days
  after** the end of each reporting period. A typical final period covers project
  months 31–60.
- **ERC scientific reports** — also within 60 days after each scientific
  reporting period ends.

Sources:
- European Research Council, "Project Reporting" (page current as of
  25 Aug 2026), https://erc.europa.eu/manage-your-project/project-reporting —
  60-day deadline, separate scientific and financial reporting periods,
  simultaneous final submission, continuous reporting.
- EU Funding & Tenders "Reports and payment requests" online manual and the
  official Horizon/Euratom Periodic Report template — Part A / Part B structure
  and Financial Statements.

## Cross-funder drafting rules

- **Tie every accomplishment to a stated aim / objective / deliverable.** Reviewers
  read the report against the funded workplan.
- **Report deviations explicitly.** EU Part B is *built* around differences;
  NIH Section F and NSF reports both expect changes/problems to be named, not
  hidden.
- **Keep report scope correct.** NSF final report = final funded year only; NIH
  Final RPPR adds Outcomes; EU final periodic report covers its final period.
- **Never fabricate numbers or products.** Leave `[TODO: confirm]` placeholders for
  any figure, date, person-month, publication, or DOI the user has not supplied.
