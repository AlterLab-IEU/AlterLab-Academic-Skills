# Peer Mentoring and First-Year Retention Intention in Engineering Faculties (revised manuscript, round 2)

*Fictional manuscript written for a review exercise. The study, authors, cited studies and data are invented.*

## Abstract

First-year attrition is high in engineering. We surveyed N = 368 first-year engineering
students at two universities to examine whether a peer-mentoring programme affects their
intention to stay. Peer mentoring increases retention intention (β = .29, p < .001), and the
effect is stronger for first-generation students. Faculties should make peer mentoring
compulsory for all first-year students.

## 1. Introduction

Students who leave engineering in the first year rarely return to it. Peer mentoring pairs a
first-year student with a trained senior student who meets them every two weeks. Recent
studies report that structured peer mentoring is associated with higher belonging and
persistence in STEM programmes (Kaya & Demir, 2022; Öztürk, 2023; Yılmaz et al., 2024).

## 2. Method

### 2.1 Participants

All 1,020 first-year engineering students at two public universities were invited in
November 2025; 412 responded. Participation in the mentoring programme was voluntary and
self-selected (mentored n = 171). Using multiple imputation, all 412 respondents were retained
in the analyses.

### 2.2 Measures

Retention intention was measured with three items (1–5). Academic engagement was measured with
a 9-item scale; in this sample Cronbach's α = .89, and a confirmatory factor analysis supported
the one-factor structure (CFI = .96, RMSEA = .05, SRMR = .04).

### 2.3 Missing data

Item non-response was low (0.9% of all item responses, in 44 respondents). Missing items were
imputed by multiple imputation with chained equations (m = 20), assuming data missing at
random; estimates were pooled with Rubin's rules.

## 4. Discussion

Peer mentoring increases first-year engineering students' intention to stay by about 0.4
points, and it helps first-generation students most. Because the programme causes higher
retention intention, faculties should make it compulsory.

**Limitations.** The study was conducted at two universities.
