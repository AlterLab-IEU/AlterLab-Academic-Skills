# Remote Work and Self-Rated Productivity among University Administrative Staff (Methods and Results)

## 2. Method

### 2.1 Participants and procedure

The population was the 1,450 administrative staff of three public universities in one city.
In March 2026 we e-mailed an invitation with a survey link to the staff mailing list of each
university and sent one reminder. Of the 243 people who opened the survey, 212 completed it
(response rate 14.6% of the population); the 31 partially completed surveys were deleted.
Staff who worked from home at least two days a week were classed as the remote group
(n = 97); the others formed the office group (n = 115). Participation was voluntary; the
ethics committee of the first author's university approved the study.

### 2.2 Measures

Productivity was measured with a 10-item self-rated productivity scale adapted by the authors
from an industry questionnaire and translated into Turkish for this study. Items were rated
from 1 (strongly disagree) to 5 (strongly agree) and summed (range 10–50). We also recorded
gender, age group (four bands), years in post, unit type (academic or central
administration), number of children at home, commuting time and job grade.

### 2.3 Analysis

Group differences were tested with independent-samples t-tests. To explore who benefits most
from remote work, we repeated the remote-versus-office comparison within subgroups. We then
fitted an OLS regression of productivity on remote status and the seven covariates.
Significance was set at p < .05. Analyses were run in SPSS 29.

## 3. Results

Remote staff reported higher productivity than office staff (remote M = 38.4, SD = 6.1;
office M = 36.2, SD = 7.9), t(240) = 2.29, p = .023.

Remote work significantly increased productivity among staff with children at home
(p = .048), but not in the other subgroups.

In the regression (R² = .18), remote status was a significant predictor (B = 0.41, p = .03):
working remotely increases productivity by 0.4 points after controlling for all covariates.

## 4. Discussion (excerpt)

Remote work causes higher productivity, especially for parents. Universities should
therefore allow all administrative staff with children to work from home at least two days a
week.
