# Competency-based assessment in an undergraduate media-production program: a mixed-methods evaluation

**Background.** Our faculty replaced letter-graded assignments with competency-based assessment
(CBA) in four studio courses in 2024–25: students had to demonstrate twelve production
competencies, with unlimited reassessment within the term.

**Methods.** Quasi-experimental comparison of the 2023–24 cohort (traditional grading, n = 118)
and the 2024–25 cohort (CBA, n = 124). Outcomes: external jury scores of final projects (blind,
two raters, ICC = .81), course completion, and a validated self-efficacy scale (pre/post).
Semi-structured interviews with 18 students and 6 instructors, analysed thematically.

**Results.** Jury scores were higher under CBA (M = 3.71 vs 3.42 on a 5-point scale; Hedges'
g = 0.41, 95% CI [0.15, 0.66]). Completion rose from 86% to 93%. Self-efficacy gains were larger
under CBA (g = 0.35). Interviews described reassessment as lowering fear of failure but raising
instructor workload by an estimated 20–30%; students with part-time jobs used reassessment least.

**Discussion.** CBA was associated with better final work and completion, but cohorts differ in
time and instructors, so the design cannot establish causation. Workload and unequal access to
reassessment are the main implementation risks.
