# Decision letter (synthetic test fixture)

**Journal:** Journal of Example Teaching Research (fictional) · **Decision:** Major revision

Dear Dr. Example,

Two reviewers have assessed your manuscript "Weekly Low-Stakes Quizzing and Final-Exam
Performance in an Introductory Statistics Course". Both see value in a classroom test of
retrieval practice, but both question the causal claims. Please respond point by point.

— The Handling Editor

## Reviewer 1

The study addresses a practical question, and the common final exam is a strength of the design.

1. The sections were not randomized and had different instructors, so instructor and section are
   confounded with the intervention. The abstract and discussion state that quizzing *caused* the
   higher scores; this language is not supported by the design.
2. Prior GPA was collected but not used. Please re-analyse the final-exam scores adjusting for
   prior GPA (ANCOVA or regression) and report the adjusted difference.

## Reviewer 2

1. The claim that the effect "will generalize to every STEM course" goes far beyond a single
   statistics course in one semester. Please remove or substantially qualify it.
2. Twelve students who withdrew were excluded. Please report how many students withdrew from each
   section, and discuss whether differential attrition could explain the difference.
