# Weekly Low-Stakes Quizzing and Final-Exam Performance in an Introductory Statistics Course: A Two-Section Quasi-Experiment

*Synthetic manuscript for workflow testing. Authors: A. Example and B. Placeholder, Department of Psychology, Example State University (fictional).*

## Abstract

Retrieval practice is among the best-supported learning techniques, but evidence from intact
university courses is thinner than evidence from the laboratory. We compared two sections of the
same introductory statistics course in one semester. One section (n = 64) took a ten-item online
quiz at the end of every week; the other (n = 58) re-read worked examples for the same time. On
the common final exam, the quizzed section scored 7.9 points higher on a 100-point scale
(Cohen's d = 0.45, 95% CI 0.09 to 0.81). **Weekly quizzing caused the higher exam scores, and
the effect will generalize to every STEM course.** We recommend that all introductory STEM
courses adopt weekly quizzing.

## 1. Introduction

Testing oneself on material, rather than restudying it, improves later retention. Roediger and
Karpicke (2006) showed that retrieval practice doubled final-exam grades in university statistics
courses [1]. Effect sizes in educational research are often interpreted with conventional
benchmarks, under which d = 0.2, 0.5 and 0.8 are small, medium and large effects [2]. A recent
multi-site replication reported that spaced retrieval schedules raised introductory-course exam
scores by 12 points across 14 universities [3]. Few studies, however, have tested weekly quizzing
in an intact statistics course with a common final exam.

## 2. Method

**Design.** Two sections of PSY 210 (Introductory Statistics) taught in the same semester were
assigned to conditions by the department scheduler: the Monday–Wednesday section received weekly
quizzes and the Tuesday–Thursday section re-read worked examples. Students were not randomized.
Section A was taught by Instructor 1 and Section B by Instructor 2, using the same syllabus,
slides and textbook.

**Participants.** 122 students consented (quiz section n = 64; re-read section n = 58). Prior GPA
was recorded from the registrar but was not used in the analysis.

**Intervention.** The quiz section completed ten multiple-choice items online each Friday, with
feedback. The re-read section spent about 20 minutes each Friday re-reading worked examples.

**Outcome.** The common final exam (100 points) was written by both instructors and graded by
teaching assistants who knew each student's section.

**Analysis.** We compared mean final-exam scores with an independent-samples t-test and report
Cohen's d with a 95% confidence interval. No covariates were included. Twelve students who
withdrew before the final exam (quiz section 4, re-read section 8) were excluded.

## 3. Results

The quiz section scored M = 78.4 (SD = 17.1) and the re-read section M = 70.5 (SD = 18.0);
t(120) = 2.48, p = .015, d = 0.45 (95% CI 0.09 to 0.81). Students in the quiz section completed
a mean of 11.2 of 13 quizzes.

## 4. Discussion

Weekly quizzing caused the higher exam scores: the quiz section outperformed the re-read section
by almost eight points, a medium effect by conventional benchmarks [2]. Because retrieval
practice works in the laboratory [1], we expect the effect to generalize to every STEM course.
Limitations: the sections had different instructors and were not randomized, and the graders were
not blind to section.

## 5. Conclusion

Weekly low-stakes quizzing should be adopted in all introductory STEM courses.
