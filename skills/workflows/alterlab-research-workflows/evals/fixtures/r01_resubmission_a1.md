## Specific Aims

Mobility disability — the inability to walk 400 m — affects one in four adults over 70 and is
more common among low-income older adults. Structured physical activity can prevent it, but
facility-based programs rarely reach low-income neighborhoods. We propose a pragmatic trial of
peer-led neighborhood walking groups.

**Aim 1.** Test whether 24 months of peer-led walking groups (three 45-minute walks a week)
reduce the incidence of major mobility disability compared with a health-education control
in 600 adults aged 70–89 with low income. *Hypothesis:* incidence will be 25% lower in the
walking-group arm.

**Aim 2.** Test whether the walking groups improve gait speed and the Short Physical
Performance Battery (SPPB) score at 12 and 24 months in a mechanistic sub-study of 180
participants.

**Aim 3.** Estimate the cost-effectiveness of the walking groups from the payer perspective
(cost per disability-free year gained).

## Research Strategy (abridged)

### A. Significance

Mobility disability predicts nursing-home admission and death. A large facility-based trial
showed that structured physical activity reduces major mobility disability, but participants
were mostly middle-income and lived near the study centers. No trial has tested a low-cost,
neighborhood-based format in low-income communities, where disability rates are highest.

### B. Innovation

Walks are led by trained older-adult peer leaders from the same neighborhood, start from
senior centers and public-housing buildings, and need no equipment or facility.

### C. Approach

**C.1 Design and recruitment.** Two-arm randomized trial. Participants are randomized
individually (1:1) and then attend walking groups of 10–12 people formed in their neighborhood;
control participants attend monthly health-education sessions. **C.1.3 Alternative recruitment
strategy:** if enrolment falls below 80% of target at month 9, we will add mailings through
Medicaid managed-care plans and extend recruitment by six months.

**C.2.2 Fidelity.** A research assistant rates a 12-item fidelity checklist on a random 20% of
sessions; leaders scoring below 80% receive booster training.

**C.2.3 Outcomes.** Major mobility disability is assessed every 6 months by a 400 m walk test by
assessors blind to arm. Gait speed and SPPB are measured at baseline, 12 and 24 months.

**C.2.4 Sample size.** For Aim 1, 600 participants give 80% power (two-sided α = .05) to detect a
hazard ratio of 0.75, assuming a 30% 24-month incidence in the control arm and 15% attrition.
The Aim 2 sub-study will enroll 180 participants, which is adequate for this analysis.

**C.3 Analysis.** Aim 1: Cox proportional-hazards model of time to major mobility disability
with arm as the predictor, analysed at the individual level. Aim 2: linear mixed models for
gait speed and SPPB with a random intercept for participant. Aim 3: a Markov model over a
10-year horizon.

**C.4 Preliminary data.** A 12-week pilot with 46 participants in two neighborhoods retained 87%
and achieved 78% session attendance.

### Investigators and Environment

PI: geriatrician with three completed NIA-funded trials. Co-I: biostatistician (trial design).
Consultant: health economist (2% effort, for Aim 3). The coordinating center has run four
multisite aging trials.
